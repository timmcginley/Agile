## A1 - How to Grow a Tent?

|          |  How to Grow a Prototype   |
|----      |-----|
| Aim      | Learn Agile Prototyping Methods with a simple example  |
| Tools    | Pen and paper|
| Case     | Tent and existing building |
| Groups   | (Group) 4 People |
| Practice | In class activity in week 1-3|
| Learning objectives | 1.1, 1.2 |
| Output   | Drawings & Models |
| Submit   | Documented process, Digital drawings or photos / scans of physical drawings |


> 1.1 Identify the ‘phenotypic’ features of a building system or product.

> 1.2 Reverse engineer the pseudo biological developmental stages of the system based on the identified features to transform the building system or product into an ‘Agile Prototype' (AP).


In this assignment you will follow the Agile Prototyping Method on your tent and building in the following steps:

# Part 1 (L1.1)
Follow the in class steps and submit of both the *tent and building*:

1. Your identified prototype features and growth stages.
2. Your identified prototype functions.
3. Your future prototype (reversed engineered)
4. Your identified needs.
    * Analyse existing building models of building 308
    * Identify needs of building and building requirements 
6. Documenting your weeks activities, features and functions.

<!-- Week 2 Activity 1 for tent and Week 3 Activity 1 for building-->
# Part 2 (L1.2)
1. Your 'How to grow a prototype' process
2. Documenting your developmental stages of your prototype

<!-- Week 3 Activity 2 for building-->
# Part 3 (L1.1 )
Part of this assignment you will follow the Step 1 of the Agile Prototyping Method, the Past: reflecting on the past to shape the present and future

This will be on your chosen system following the in class acitivites and submit:

1. Identify one thing (system) that is missing from your tent to make it into a ‘complete’ building.
2. On campus, identify three examples of that thing that you feel represent three steps in the evolutional history of that system
3. Identify the phenotypic plasticity of these systems – your agile prototypes 
4. Identify the ‘evolutionary history’ of your agile prototypes
